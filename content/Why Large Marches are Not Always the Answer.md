It's 2025 and people are calling for large marches and protests against Donald Trump again. We did this in 2016 during his first term and frankly the large marches, especially the Women's March, felt like they sapped the energy from people. Once it was over, what did the march build or provoke? What was the purpose of marching then and do we need to do it now?

Marches can show [[Solidarity]] and that a lot of people care about an issue. This was true during the U.S. Civil Rights era and is still true today. Large marches also helped show the U.S. government that people were opposed to U.S. intervention in Vietnam.

Marches can also build excitement and encourage more people to get involved as described in [[hayes.kaba2023 | Let This Radicalize You]]. The energy of working towards a common goal with many other people can be electric. Large marches may also activate people who felt alone in their views on the subject of the march, by letting them know other people care deeply about the topic. But that's only if they hear about the march, which is less and less likely from corporate media.

The lack of mention about large marches in corporate media lessens it's impact, because part of the reason to hold a march is to show uninvolved people that the subject of the march matters. If media doesn't talk about the march then the power of marching has been diminished. Media may also mischaracterize what the march was about, just like with protests during Spring 2024 about Israel's genocide in Gaza, which many media outlets said was due to antisemitism and not against genocide.

Vincent Bevins' book [[bevins2023 | If we burn: the mass protest decade and the missing revolution]] describes how protests and mass movements, if they don't have a plan for the future, can keep the status quo or lead to worse outcomes if more organized actors (with right-wing views) take charge. 

[[bevins2023 | Bevins]] argues that protests should not be the only way to organize against issues, since other methods like strikes and boycotts can be more effective, and that if there is a protest, the organizers need to have a clear vision and organization. This is somewhat similar to the call for deep organizing that goes beyond calling out people for marches in McAlevey's book [[mcalevey2016 | No Shortcuts]].
